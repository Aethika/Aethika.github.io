Using the Governance Effectiveness Diagnostic Workbench

The Governance Effectiveness Diagnostic Workbench helps boards, executives and governance practitioners assess whether governance and ethics are operating as a decision-useful, coherent and accountable system across the Business Operating Model.

The Workbench is designed around an outcomes-first diagnostic approach. It does not start by asking whether every practice, standard or policy exists. It asks whether governance is actually helping the organisation make better decisions, preserve accountability, operate ethically, create sustainable value and maintain legitimacy.

Interpretation boundary: The Workbench is a diagnostic and decision-support tool. It is not a certification, assurance product, legal opinion, compliance assessment, or official interpretation of any governance code or responsible business framework.

Quick start

  1. Open the Workbench. Review the overview, terms, privacy notice and known limitations.
  2. Complete the Pre-Assessment. Use either Guided View or Table View to respond to the 30 executive indicators.
  3. Review Results. Interpret the five-lens heatmap, primary attention lens, Governance Effectiveness Model and recommended diagnostic pathway.
  4. Export the Pre-Assessment Report. Use the report as a discussion artefact, not as a certification or final conclusion.
  5. Move into the Diagnostic where appropriate. The Diagnostic examines the indicators triggered by weak pre-assessment responses and turns them into issue patterns, observations and recommendations.

Before sharing reports: Review them for sensitive governance, legal, stakeholder, human rights, AI, board or assurance information. The Workbench runs locally, but exported reports are files that users control.

Core concepts

The five diagnostic lenses

The Workbench assesses governance effectiveness through five lenses. Each lens looks for outcome signals rather than standards coverage alone.

1 — Proportionality

Whether governance effort is proportionate, coordinated and decision-useful, or whether governance burden is absorbed through duplication, fragmentation, unclear ownership and low decision value.

2 — Line of Sight

Whether purpose, strategy, obligations, risks, controls, decisions, AI influence, stakeholder impacts, outcomes, escalation and remedy can be traced across the Business Operating Model.

3 — Value Realisation

Whether governance creates, preserves and protects value without eroding ethical conduct, human dignity, stakeholder trust or organisational legitimacy.

4 — Decision Velocity

Whether the right decisions can move at the right pace without bypassing ethical judgement, stakeholder consideration, risk thresholds, escalation or accountability.

5 — Accountability

Whether decisions, delegations, controls, stakeholder trade-offs, adverse impacts, remedy pathways and AI-influenced outcomes can be explained, owned, challenged, assured and improved.

AI as a cross-cutting overlay

AI is not treated as a narrow technology topic. It is a whole-of-governance operating condition because AI can change authority, accountability, judgement, explainability, stakeholder impact, risk propagation, assurance and remedy.

  • Authority: AI can influence who or what effectively makes a decision.
  • Accountability: human ownership must remain clear when AI informs, recommends, automates or constrains action.
  • Explainability and contestability: affected parties and accountable decision-makers may need to understand, challenge or override outcomes.
  • Stakeholder impact: AI can scale consequences across customers, workers, communities, suppliers and other affected groups.

Governance Effectiveness Model

The results screen positions the assessment on the Governance Effectiveness Model using two axes:

AxisBuilt fromWhat it means
Governance CoherenceLine of Sight + AccountabilityWhether governance can be traced, explained, owned and improved across the Business Operating Model.
Decision ConfidenceProportionality + Value Realisation + Decision VelocityWhether governance enables timely, informed, ethical and accountable decisions.

The quadrants are Governance Drag, Controlled but Slow, Fast but Exposed and Governed Performance. The model is directional and should be validated through discussion, evidence review and, where appropriate, a more detailed diagnostic.

Pre-Assessment

The Pre-Assessment is an executive-level signal scan. It consists of 30 positively framed indicators across the five lenses. It is intended to identify where governance and ethics appear strong, mixed or weak enough to justify deeper diagnostic work.

Views

Guided View

Moves through the indicators one at a time. Use this mode for facilitated discussions, board workshops or executive interviews.

Table View

Shows all indicators in a rapid completion format. Use this mode when entering responses quickly or reviewing a completed response set.

Pre-Assessment response scale

The Pre-Assessment is positively framed. Higher scores mean stronger governance and ethics effectiveness.

ResponseScoreInterpretation
SD0Not visible, not addressed, or ineffective.
D1Weak, informal, or person-dependent.
N2Partially present, inconsistent, or difficult to determine.
A3Generally effective and operationally meaningful.
SA4Integrated, adaptive, and decision-useful.

Trigger rule: Indicators scored below Neutral, meaning SD or D, are selected for diagnostic review. Neutral responses do not trigger the Diagnostic by default.

Interpreting Pre-Assessment results

  • Five-lens heatmap: shows the relative signal profile across the five lenses.
  • Primary Attention Lens: the weakest lens based on numeric score.
  • Secondary Attention Lens: the next distinct lowest-scoring lens, or multiple secondary lenses where there is a tie.
  • Governance Effectiveness Model: plots Governance Coherence and Decision Confidence.
  • Recommended pathway: suggests where the Diagnostic should focus first.

Diagnostic

The Diagnostic is a deeper, issue-focused assessment of selected indicators. It is usually generated from the pre-assessment indicators that scored below Neutral, but access and scope may depend on the active licence and configuration.

Unlike the Pre-Assessment, the Diagnostic is issue-framed. Its statements describe observable gaps, delays, burdens, weaknesses, disconnects or failure patterns.

Diagnostic response scale

In the Diagnostic, higher scores mean stronger evidence that the issue is present or persistent. Do not interpret a high Diagnostic score as a strong governance condition.

ResponseScoreInterpretation
SD0Low issue signal or issue not observed.
D1Limited or emerging issue signal.
N2Mixed, unclear or inconsistent issue signal.
A3Operationally meaningful issue signal.
SA4Structural or persistent issue signal.

Diagnostic statements

Each diagnostic indicator is typically assessed through three statements:

  • Presence: whether the issue is visible.
  • Persistence: whether the issue recurs or persists across contexts.
  • Dependency: whether the organisation depends on workarounds, manual intervention or compensating activity.

Patterns and recommendations

The Diagnostic uses pattern logic to identify recurring conditions across indicators. Patterns should be interpreted as structured prompts for further evidence review, not as final findings. Recommendations should be reviewed in light of organisational context, risk appetite, legal obligations, stakeholder expectations and available capability.

Reports and exports

Pre-Assessment Report

The Executive Pre-Assessment Report summarises the directional signal across the five lenses, identifies the primary attention lens, shows the Governance Effectiveness Model and recommends a next diagnostic pathway.

Diagnostic Report

The Diagnostic Report summarises issue signals, triggered patterns, recommendations, proof metrics and interpretation boundaries. It should be used to support discussion, scoping and prioritisation.

Privacy and export handling

The Workbench runs locally. Responses are not uploaded, shared or transmitted by the application. However, exported PDF reports are files controlled by the user. Treat them as sensitive governance artefacts.

  • Review exported reports before sharing.
  • Avoid entering confidential board deliberations, legal privilege material, whistleblower information or personal data unless authorised.
  • Store reports according to your organisation’s records, privacy and information security policies.

Troubleshooting

The app is showing old lens names or old wording

Confirm that the app is loading the latest packaged configuration. If a previously imported configuration is active, switch to the packaged configuration or import the latest GEDiagnosticWorkbench.dwconfig.

Results do not match the workbook

Check whether saved state was created under an older configuration version. If the active configuration version or checksum has changed, old responses may need to be reset. The Workbench should not silently reuse stale responses against an incompatible configuration.

No diagnostic indicators are triggered

In the Pre-Assessment, only responses below Neutral trigger Diagnostic scope by default. If all responses are N, A or SA, no Diagnostic indicators are triggered unless the configuration defines a different rule.

The 2x2 dot looks wrong

All Neutral responses should place the dot at the centre intersection: Governance Coherence 50% and Decision Confidence 50%. If this is not happening, confirm that the latest configuration is loaded and that no stale state is being reused.

PDF export contains old labels

Legacy lens, axis or prior-methodology terminology indicates stale configuration or outdated application code. Regenerate the configuration from the current workbooks and reload the app in packaged configuration mode.
