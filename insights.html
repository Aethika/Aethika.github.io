Corporate governance in the age of AI

AI is not only a tool-level governance problem. It changes how decisions are made, how authority is embedded, and how evidence must be produced. The board question shifts from “who approved the decision?” to “who approved the conditions under which the system could keep deciding?”

Governance is no longer a backdrop

Governance codes and principles remain essential, but they need infrastructure that allows them to operate where decisions are made. Governance becomes a practical capability for bringing principles, boundaries, and accountability into judgement as it happens.

Corporate governance in the age of autonomous execution

Corporate conduct rules were designed for human-paced execution. Autonomous systems and AI-mediated decisions require harm anticipation, continuous oversight, and translation of conduct expectations into system behaviour.

AI governance by design

The playbook gives each lifecycle stage practical activities, checklists, templates, and controls, allowing governance to scale by risk while generating evidence progressively.

A unified approach to governance

AI governance should not become a parallel structure. Enterprise governance defines the rules, project governance delivers change, AI governance safeguards decision behaviour, and GRC tooling provides the control backbone.
